# How Nurture becomes part of someone's life

**Purpose:** to define how Nurture earns a durable place in a person's daily life — the promise, the
borrowed mechanics, the signature experiences and the order to build them.

**Scope and limitations:** this is a strategy document, not a specification of shipped behaviour. It
cites general research themes rather than individual studies, and it makes no quantitative claims.
Any figure referenced here must be traced to a properly cited source before publication, and reward
and revenue ideas are described as **design intent**, not as guarantees.

## The core promise and the 20-second test

The promise is unchanged from the product blueprint: *"Help me make the next healthy choice that
fits my real life."* The test of whether the product keeps that promise is the **20-second test** —
a user should be able to open Nurture and know **what to do next within 20 seconds**, without reading
menus, without a tutorial and without being shown a dashboard of numbers first.

## Lessons to borrow from successful platforms

| Platform | The mechanic worth borrowing | Nurture equivalent |
|---|---|---|
| Duolingo | A clear daily action, flexible streaks, friend streaks, streak freeze | One next good step; flexible streaks that include planned rest; a 30-second restart |
| Strava | Shared activity and group challenges without a leaderboard | Small group challenges and the Circle of Care; comparison against your own baseline first |
| Finch | A caring companion tied to small self-care actions | A coach that checks in gently and ties care to tiny actions |
| TikTok | Fast, interest-shaped discovery | A **finite**, health-relevant daily set — discovery without an endless feed |
| Pinterest | Saving and organising ideas into usable plans | Saved recipes and ideas that flow into the meal planner and groceries |
| Instagram | Creator connection, polls, broadcast updates | Creator programmes with live events and polls, private by default |
| Discord | Smaller moderated communities with roles and scheduled events | Moderated communities with roles and scheduled creator events |
| Apple Watch | Simple activity feedback and opt-in friendly competition | Lock-screen and watch quick actions; opt-in, non-shaming challenges |
| YouTube | Creators who can build a sustainable business | A creator revenue share with published terms |

## The signature experience

Three things make Nurture feel like a companion rather than an app. First, **one Today screen** that
answers "what now?". Second, a **coach that understands why a plan fails** — not just that a day was
missed, but the pattern behind it (late shifts, a rough week, a festival, exam season). Third, a
**plan that adapts without becoming unpredictable**: adaptation is announced, explained and approved,
so the user always knows the shape of the week.

Life modes make adaptation concrete: **travel, shift work, illness, festival, exam week, new parent
and low budget**. Each mode changes suggestions — a hotel-room session instead of a gym routine, a
night-shift eating rhythm, a low-effort "rough week" plan — while keeping the plan recognisable.

## The Commitment Dial

The dial lets the user choose how much support they want. It changes **how Nurture prompts**, never
what Nurture demands of the body.

| Level | Exactly what it does | Boundaries |
|---|---|---|
| Gentle | Occasional soft nudges; easy to ignore; no follow-up pressure | Never shames; can be paused |
| Structured | Timed prompts tied to the daily rhythm and the weekly review | Never comments on weight or calories |
| Accountability | Checks in on a commitment the user chose for themselves | Strictness applies to a chosen **process**, never to the body; can be lowered or paused |

The non-negotiables for every level: **never shames**, **never comments on weight or calories**, and
**can always be paused**. Strictness is about following a process the user selected, not about
measuring the body.

## Profit sharing and rewards

These are **three separate products** and must never be blurred into one promise:

1. **Member rewards** — points and badges funded from a **clearly funded pool**, with published
   rules and caps. Illustrative only; no cash value in the demo.
2. **Creator revenue share** — creators earn from their programmes under **published terms** that
   state how the split works and when it is paid.
3. **A genuine member dividend or ownership share** — a real share of value returned to members,
   which **requires a legal and financial structure first**.

The rule is plain: **guaranteed earnings must never be advertised before that structure exists.**
Until the legal and financial vehicle is in place, any dividend or ownership language stays a stated
ambition, not a promise.

## Idea inventory

A full backlog of ideas, grouped by area. Each is a candidate, not a commitment.

**Daily life and convenience** — morning briefing; calendar-aware routine; lock-screen and watch
quick actions; voice capture; offline day plan; shared household planning; and the life modes
(travel, shift-work, exam, new-parent and low-budget).

**Movement** — a five-minute replacement session for a day that collapsed; "what should I do at the
gym today?"; and accessible movement goals that **do not assume step counting**.

**Food and nutrition** — photo logging with an editable estimate and a confidence label; barcode and
saved-meal logging; pantry mode; leftover and batch cooking; meal plans by culture, taste, cost,
time and allergy; "why this meal fits today"; flexible swaps; restaurant and festival guidance with
**no moral labels**; hands-free cooking mode; short nutrition lessons; and a less-number-focused mode.

**Sleep, recovery and wellbeing** — a wind-down plan; stress check-ins; and a "rough week" mode.

**Motivation and identity** — a private Progress Passport; a "why I started" card; ability-based
milestones; a 30-second restart; flexible streaks that include planned rest; and seasonal chapters.

**Community and comparison** — a private Circle of Care of one to five people; granular sharing;
small group challenges; comparison against your own baseline first; **no default public rankings**;
moderated communities; and live creator events.

**AI experience** — an AI Memory Ledger the user can see and delete; "explain this recommendation";
"give me three alternatives"; confidence labels; weekly review with approval; and distinct coach
tones.

**Trust and control** — private by default; per-connection consent; export and delete; a record of
**which data produced a recommendation**; notification controls; accessibility and food-culture
coverage; and a way to report a harmful suggestion.

## Three signature experiences

**The Life-Aware Day.** Amara has a 07:00 train and a late meeting. Opening Nurture she sees one
step — a ten-minute walk at the station — and a note that lunch is a travel day, so the meal plan
offers something she can buy, not cook. When the meeting overruns, she taps a life mode and the
evening wind-down shifts without guilt. Nothing is lost; the week simply rebalances.

**The Comeback.** After a rough week, Sam has missed five days and expects a scolding. Instead
there is a 30-second restart: one small action, a note that missed days are information, and a
flexible streak that shows the planned rest as part of the record. The plan is not reset to zero.

**The Shared Journey.** Priya's Circle of Care is her partner and a friend. They see a small group
challenge and her personal progress, but no public ranking and no numbers she did not choose to
share. Her supporters send encouragement from a set of message ideas rather than commenting on her
body.

## What to build first, in order

1. **The 20-second Today screen** — the single next step, with a reason and an alternative.
2. **The daily loop** — morning briefing, logging, evening check-in, weekly review.
3. **Adaptation with approval** — life modes and the human-in-the-loop plan review.
4. **The Commitment Dial** — gentle, structured, accountability, with pausing.
5. **Trust surface** — export, delete, per-connection consent, notification controls.
6. **Community and rewards** — Circle of Care, small challenges, funded rewards with published terms.

## How to judge success

Success signals: users can name their next step quickly; plans survive a bad week; people return after
rest without shame; and the weekly review is approved rather than ignored. **Anti-metrics** — not
success on their own — are time-in-app, streak length and notification volume; heavy usage and long
streaks can signal compulsion, not health.

The signals that must **trigger a safety review**: a rise in notification opt-outs; privacy
complaints; reports of harmful suggestions; and any report of obsessive tracking or disordered
eating. Each of these should pause feature work and route to clinical and privacy review.

## Research basis

This strategy draws on **general research themes** rather than specific studies, and any publication
of these claims must cite the underlying work properly:

- Personalised goals, reminders and some form of human support tend to help people change health
  behaviours.
- Engagement with an app does **not by itself** produce better health outcomes; usage is not the
  same as benefit.
- Gamification tends to produce **modest average benefits**, with wide variation between people.
- Usage commonly **declines over time**, which is normal and should be designed for rather than
  fought.
- Fitness and diet tracking can be **associated with disordered-eating symptoms in some people**,
  and **social comparison can cause harm** — both are reasons for the boundaries in this document.

These are described as themes only. **Specific studies must be cited properly before publication;
no paper titles, authors or URLs are asserted here.**
